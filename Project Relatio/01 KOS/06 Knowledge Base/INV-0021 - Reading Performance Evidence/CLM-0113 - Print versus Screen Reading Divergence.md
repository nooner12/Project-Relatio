# CLM-0113

# Print vs Screen Reading Divergence (CLM-E)

## Draft Claim Record

---

# Claim
> **Measured reading performance does differ by medium in this base's read meta-analysis — a paper advantage for comprehension (Hedges' g = −.21 across 54 studies and 171,055 participants, identical in between- and within-participant designs) that is moderated by time constraints (larger under time pressure) and text genre (present for informational and mixed texts, absent for narrative-only), has INCREASED from 2000 to 2017 rather than dissolving with better screens, and does NOT vary by age group from elementary school through college — while the medium-specific typography prescriptions in this base (different point sizes for print and web, read from the practitioner artifact) are stated without derivation from any of this measured divergence at the surfaces accessible this session. The measured divergence is about comprehension under cognitively demanding conditions; the medium-specific size prescriptions are about visual rendering — the two literatures pass each other without touching in this base.**

---

# Element (i) — The Prescriptive Approach (as stated in the practice literature)

- **Medium-differentiated size rules (SRC-0194, live-read):** 10–12 points for print documents, 15–25 pixels for the web — an explicit medium split in the practitioner prescription set.
- Screen-specific platform prescriptions exist in the vendor artifact and are carried in CLM-0114's territory; the print-vs-screen split itself is the (i) content here.

# Element (ii) — The Stated Rationale

- The read practitioner summary page states the medium-split rules without stating their reason on that surface; the stated-rationale pole for the medium split is **thin at the surfaces accessible this session** — bounded (the book's other pages are the disclosed hole).

# Element (iii) — Measured Human-Performance Evidence (including explicit bounded absence)

- **The read meta-analysis (SRC-0191, interior READ — author-hosted full text):** 2000–2017 studies of comparable texts on paper versus digital devices; 38 between-participant plus 16 within-participant designs; 54 studies, 76 comparisons, 171,055 participants. Verbatim: "Both designs yielded the same advantage of paper over digital reading (Hedge's g = -.21; dc = -.21)." Three significant moderators, verbatim: "(1) time frame: the paper-based reading advantage increased in time-constrained reading compared to self-paced reading; (2) text genre: the paper-based reading advantage was consistent across studies using informational texts, or a mix of informational and narrative texts, but not on those using only narrative texts; (3) publication year: the advantage of paper-based reading increased over the years."
- **Population sub-element (SRC-0191, read):** media differences "do not vary according to … age group (educational level)" — the paper advantage holds from elementary school through college; it is not a child-specific or adult-specific phenomenon in this corpus.
- **Magnitude framing (SRC-0191, read):** the effect "represent[s] approximately 2/3 of the yearly growth in comprehension in elementary school, and 1/2 of the effect of remedial interventions."
- **The era anchor (SRC-0190, interior unread this session; abstract-establishable):** the 1992 critical review found the early literature dominated by image-quality-driven speed deficits and concluded single-variable explanations insufficient — the historical frame within which the modern comprehension-level divergence sits. (The open repository copy returned 403 to this session's fetches; access failure disclosed.)
- **EXPLICITLY ABSENT at the surfaces accessible this session:** any measured derivation connecting the medium-specific size/face/contrast prescriptions to the measured comprehension divergence; and modern speed/visual-performance comparisons by medium in-base (the read meta is comprehension-outcome). Unread interiors (SRC-0190; the practitioner book's interior) are the disclosed holes.

# Element (iv) — Documented Costs and Failure Modes (from the sources; not inferred)

- **Time-pressure amplification (SRC-0191, read):** the paper advantage grows under time constraints — the documented condition under which treating media as equivalent costs most.
- **Genre boundary (SRC-0191, read):** narrative-only reading shows no media effect — the documented limit of the divergence (a medium-equivalence prescription is wrong for informational reading and unsupported-but-unharmed for narrative in this corpus).
- **The trend against obsolescence (SRC-0191, read):** the advantage increased from 2000 to 2017 — the documented failure of the assumption that improving display technology dissolves the divergence.
- **Scrolling and device trends (SRC-0191, read, explicitly non-significant):** "although they did not reach significance, the results suggest stronger media differences on computers than on hand-held devices, as well as disadvantages of digital texts that require scrolling" — carried at the source's own non-significant framing.

# Limitations

# Alternative Interpretations
1. **"The paper advantage prescribes paper."** Refused — g = −.21 with genre and pacing moderators grounds no context-general medium prescription; the narrative null and self-paced attenuation are part of the same read record.
2. **"Screens have improved since 2017, so the finding is stale."** The read record's own trend runs the other way (the advantage increased with year); staleness is possible but is an assumption, and the documented trend is what the base contains.
3. **"The medium-split size prescriptions are justified by the divergence."** Not established — the prescriptions concern rendering sizes, the divergence concerns comprehension under load; no in-base source connects them, and that non-connection is the recorded finding.
