# CLM-0108

# Physiological Response Claims (CLM-F)

## Draft Claim Record

---

# Claim
> **The physiological prescription family (colors produce autonomic effects — arousal, heart rate — that branding should exploit) was the least sourceable prescription family at the surfaces accessible this session: no accessible in-base surface states it directly (the practitioner manual's unread interior is the disclosed hole this absence claim does not cover). Measured physiological evidence exists — room-scale color experiments with EEG and EKG found red environments producing a more excited cortical state with a PARADOXICAL slowing of heart rate and stronger effects in introverted or negative-mood participants — but the measured direction contradicts the folk claim's usual form, the settings are lab room interiors rather than brand assets, and the read field evidence in the adjacent red-effects family (waiter/waitress studies) runs null-to-negative. NO source in this base measures any physiological response to a brand asset: the transfer step from environmental-color physiology to branding prescription is unmeasured in this base — the element's central explicit absence.**

---

# Element (i) — The Prescriptive Approach (as stated in the practice literature)

- **The physiological prescription family could not be sourced directly at any surface accessible this session.** The base's read practitioner artifact (SRC-0178) prescribes emotional meanings, not explicitly physiological effects; **the practitioner manual (SRC-0177) is unread, and its 336-page interior is the most likely in-base location for a direct physiological prescription — this absence claim does not cover it** (F2 rebounding). The family (red raises pulse; blue lowers arousal) is documented **only indirectly**, via the measurement literature that tests color-arousal claims (SRC-0174's framing at abstract level). Recorded as a thin, indirect (i) pole at the accessible surfaces — itself a datum: the strongest-sounding claim family was the hardest to source at those surfaces. *(The often-circulated appetite variant of the claim has no in-base trail at all, even indirect, and is not asserted here — A3.)*

# Element (ii) — The Stated Rationale

- The arousal-theoretic rationale — color wavelength/saturation drives autonomic arousal, which drives behavior — is documented at abstract level in the experimental literature's own framing (SRC-0174: colors "had an impact on the emotions and physiology" of room occupants; red putting "the brain into a more excited state"). No primary practitioner statement of the rationale is in base. Low-strength, indirect.

# Element (iii) — Measured Psychological/Physiological Response Evidence (including replication status, setting, and explicit absence)

- **Direct physiological measurement (SRC-0174, interior unread; abstract-establishable):** three experiments on colored room interiors (colorful vs gray; red vs blue rooms) with EEG and EKG alongside emotional and performance measures. Reported at abstract level: strong colors and patterns, especially red, put the brain into a more excited state, **"sometimes to such an extent as to cause a paradoxical slowing of the heart rate"**; introverted participants and those in a negative mood were more affected, with consequent performance changes. **Setting: laboratory room interiors. Sample sizes: not establishable without the interior — stated.** **Replication status: no replication record in this base**; the three-experiment comparison is internal to the research program.
- **The direction problem, anchored on the source's own word:** the folk form of the claim (red accelerates heart rate) is not what the abstract reports — the reported cardiac effect is a **slowing** under strong excitation, **which the source's own abstract itself calls "paradoxical"** — i.e., the direction-against-expectation judgment is the source's, not this analyst's (A3 anchoring). The measured base's best-documented autonomic result runs against the prescription family's usual direction. (Interior unread; this rests on the abstract's own wording.)
- **Field-setting evidence in the adjacent red-effects family (SRC-0176, interior READ):** the meta-analysis's supplementary analyses report no significant red effect for men rating waitresses (d = −0.16 [−0.33, 0.01], p = .06 — described by the source as a trend toward a weak repulsive effect) and a significant negative effect for women rating a waiter (d = −0.48 [−0.82, −0.16]) — the field-setting results run null-to-negative — while facial-redness manipulations (a physiological-signal manipulation, lab setting) showed larger positive effects (men d = 0.49 [0.33, 0.65]; women d = 0.38 [0.20, 0.57]). Lab-vs-field divergence is thus **documented within the read source**. *(Remediated per Critical Review – RQ-0020 F1: an earlier wording presented a characterization in quotation marks; the figures above are the source's own.)*
- **The field's own summary (SRC-0161, abstract verbatim):** the color-response literature is at "a nascent stage," needing work on "boundary conditions, moderators, and real-world generalizability... before strong conceptual statements and recommendations for application are warranted."
- **THE CENTRAL EXPLICIT ABSENCE:** **no source in this base measures any physiological outcome (heart rate, skin conductance, EEG, or other autonomic measure) of exposure to a brand asset — a logo, a brand palette, a typeface, or a layout.** Every physiological measurement in the base is environmental (rooms) or interpersonal (clothing/faces). **The transfer step from measured environmental-color physiology to any branding prescription is unmeasured in this base — and no in-base source even asserts the transfer with evidence; it is absent at both the evidence and the citation level.**

# Element (iv) — Documented Costs and Failure Modes (from the sources; not inferred)

- **Evidence-base instability (SRC-0176, READ):** decline effect, preregistration moderator, upward-bias indications, and stimulus-specificity — documented failure modes of the best-studied red-effect family (recorded in full in CLM-0103; cross-referenced here because the physiological prescription family leans on the same program's credibility).
- **Individual-difference moderation (SRC-0174, abstract-level):** introversion and negative mood amplified color effects — a documented moderator inside the measured base (the abstract's own statement). What this implies for uniform prescriptions is an application inference and is **not drawn here**.
- **Direction inconsistency (SRC-0174, abstract-level):** the paradoxical heart-rate result is itself a documented failure mode for any prescription assuming a simple arousal direction.

# Assumptions (KOS-0003 §10)
- **The abstract of SRC-0174 is taken as the study's own summary**; the interior may qualify the paradoxical-slowing result (disclosed).
- **Environmental ≠ brand-asset setting (binding).** No room-scale result is converted into a brand-asset claim.
- **Cross-claim inheritance:** the red-effects evidence-base instability is inherited from CLM-0103's read-source analysis, cited not re-derived.

# Reasoning (KOS-0003 §7)

Risks checked: (1) **overreading one abstract's paradox** — the direction problem rests on abstract wording; graded and hedged accordingly; (2) **motivated dissolution** (the interesting result is the absence; controlled by recording the real measured physiology at full strength); (3) **program non-independence** between SRC-0161/0176, named.

# Limitations
- Asserts nothing about physiological effects outside this base; does not claim color has no physiological effects (the base documents some); claims only that the branding transfer is unmeasured here and the measured direction is inconsistent with the folk form at the accessible surface.
- **May not be cited outside Relatio or for any external reliance (R0), regardless of INV-0020's closure state.**

# Alternative Interpretations
1. **"Room-color physiology transfers to brand assets at reduced scale."** Possibly, but unmeasured in this base and asserted by no in-base source; recording it as support would be exactly the asserted-transfer step the claim exists to test.
2. **"The paradoxical heart-rate slowing is an artifact."** Possible — it is one program's abstract-level result with no in-base replication; recorded with that status, not adjudicated.
3. **"The facial-redness effects show physiological signals matter, supporting color prescriptions."** Rejected as stated — those manipulations vary a biological signal on a person, not a designed asset's color; the read source distinguishes them, and so does this record.
