# CLM-0106

# Mark Shape and Symbolism (CLM-D)

## Draft Claim Record

---

# Claim
> **The logo-design measurement literature is real, as anticipated: this base holds the foundational logo measurement study (design characteristics measured against consumer-response dimensions in the field's flagship journal) and a five-experiment program on mark roundness vs angularity (circular vs angular shapes activating softness vs hardness associations that carry into brand-attribute judgments). But in this base its measured effects are establishable only at abstract level, concern perception and judgment outcomes, and carry no replication record; and the symbolic-content claim family — that marks carry or should carry specific symbolic meanings beyond shape geometry — has NO measured base here at all: element (iii) is explicitly empty for symbolism beyond geometry.**

---

# Element (i) — The Prescriptive Approach (as stated in the practice literature)

- The shape-prescription family (round marks read as soft/friendly/harmonious, angular marks as hard/strong/dynamic; marks should symbolize the organization's values) is documented in this base **thinly and mostly indirectly**: the measurement literature's own framings (SRC-0170's title poses "Does your company have the right logo?"; SRC-0169 produces "guidelines for selecting or modifying logos") show the guideline-consuming practice exists. The practitioner manual (SRC-0177) is establishable only at publisher-surface level; its specific shape and symbolism prescriptions are **not asserted** (unread interior).
- **No primary practitioner artifact in this base states the shape-symbolism code directly** (the base's one read practitioner artifact, SRC-0178, covers color only). Recorded as a base limit on the (i) pole.

# Element (ii) — The Stated Rationale

- As stated at abstract level by the measurement literature: shapes carry semantic associations (softness/hardness per SRC-0170's theorization) that transfer to attribute judgments about the product or company; well-chosen design characteristics produce intended audience responses (SRC-0169's framing). Recorded in the design-intent register.

# Element (iii) — Measured Psychological/Physiological Response Evidence (including replication status and explicit absence)

- **Foundational logo measurement (SRC-0169, interior unread; abstract/surface-establishable):** the 1998 flagship-journal study measured consumer responses to logo design characteristics and derived empirically based selection/modification guidance. The catalog surface establishes measured response dimensions including recognition, affect, and meaning; specific effect sizes and design-dimension structure are **not asserted** (unread interior; a repository copy attempt failed on a certificate error). **Replication status: an international validation literature is known to exist at the search surface but is not in this base; within-base, no replication record.**
- **Roundness/angularity (SRC-0170, interior unread; abstract-establishable):** five experiments; the mere circularity/angularity of a logo influences product/company attribute judgments, theorized and shown via activation of softness vs hardness associations. Outcome class: attribute **judgments** (perception), not behavior or physiology. **Replication status: no replication record in this base.**
- **Symbolic content beyond geometry: EXPLICITLY EMPTY.** No source in this base measures whether marks convey specific symbolic meanings (heritage, values, narratives) beyond shape geometry, or whether such symbolism produces any response. The symbolic-content portion of the prescription family has **no measured base in this base.**
- **Population sub-elements: EXPLICITLY ABSENT.** No age, developmental, or cross-cultural evidence on mark-shape response exists in this base.

# Element (iv) — Documented Costs and Failure Modes (from the sources; not inferred)

- **EXPLICITLY EMPTY at the surfaces read.** Neither abstract documents costs or failure modes of shape prescriptions; the unread interiors may (SRC-0169's guidelines reportedly address modification risks at its surface framing, but nothing specific is establishable without the interior). The emptiness claim is therefore **weak and graded accordingly** — it binds the surfaces read, not the studies.
