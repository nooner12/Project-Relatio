# CLM-0103

# Single-Hue Response Prescriptions (CLM-A)

## Draft Claim Record

---

# Claim
> **The single-hue prescription family ("blue conveys trust, red conveys urgency/excitement, green conveys calm/health") is stated in the practice literature as a context-general selection code, with no citation trail on its most-circulated artifact; the measured literatures in this base support color-emotion ASSOCIATIONS (partly universal across 30 nations at the association level, nationally and linguistically modulated, and developmentally emergent rather than innate) but do NOT support the prescriptive code at prescriptive strength: the field's principal review states in its own abstract that the literature is at a nascent stage and that recommendations for application are not yet warranted, and the best-studied single-hue response effect in the base (the red-attraction family) survives, if at all, at small and contested magnitude — with pre-registered replications near zero, high heterogeneity, indications of upward bias, and a documented decline effect.** Everything asserted is within-base; nothing here is an application decision.

---

# Element (i) — The Prescriptive Approach (as stated in the practice literature)

- **The circulated code, from the live-read artifact (SRC-0178):** the Color Emotion Guide page prescribes hue-emotion pairings as selection guidance — yellow = optimism/clarity/warmth; orange = creativity/youthfulness/enthusiasm/confidence; red = warmth/excitement/sexiness/urgency (with energy, romance, positivity, and affection also listed); purple = royalty/imagination/wisdom; blue = calm/strength/dependability/trust/professionalism; green = serenity/growth/health; black = professional/credible; white = clean/pure. (Wording aligned to the page's own terms per A5.) The prescriptions are stated as general properties of the hues, without context conditions, population conditions, or citations.
- **Breadth of the practice:** the artifact's wide circulation is documented (independent republication from at least January 2013 onward, per the SRC-0178 catalog record). The standard practitioner manual in the base (SRC-0177, interior unread) is establishable only at its publisher-surface level — a comprehensive brand-identity manual covering design execution — and is cited here **only** as evidence that a practitioner manual literature exists; what it specifically prescribes about hue is **not asserted** (unread interior).

# Element (ii) — The Stated Rationale

Recorded in the practice literature's own register, carrying no evidential weight for element (iii):

- SRC-0178 (live-read) grounds its code in an asserted general psychology of color — that colors invoke specific emotions in viewers and that this correlation is established knowledge — presented without sources. The rationale's form is: hue → fixed emotional meaning → transferred brand perception.
- The rationale is uncited **on the artifact itself**; the citation trail terminates in assertion (the SRC-0178 trace, executed at cataloguing).

# Element (iii) — Measured Psychological/Physiological Response Evidence (including replication status and explicit absence)

**Association evidence (measured, large-sample, single study in base):** SRC-0162 (interior unread; abstract-establishable) measured color-term/emotion-concept associations in 4,598 participants across 30 nations and 22 languages, finding high cross-national similarity of association patterns (reported average similarity r = .88) with nation, language, and geographic proximity modulating them. **Scope condition carried: this measures stated associations with color TERMS, not affective, behavioral, or physiological response to presented color.** Replication status: a single large multi-nation study; no replication record in this base.

**Brand-register association evidence:** SRC-0164 (interior unread; abstract/surface-establishable) is a measured study of color and brand-personality associations in marketing (its title names two: exciting red, competent blue). Its existence establishes that brand-color association measurement exists; its specific results are not asserted (unread interior). Replication status: none recorded in this base.

**Response evidence and its own field's summary:** the principal review of measured color-response research (SRC-0161, interior unread; abstract retrieved verbatim this session) states that color "can carry important meaning and can have an important impact on people's affect, cognition, and behavior," **and in the same abstract**: "The literature remains at a nascent stage of development, however, and we note that considerable work on boundary conditions, moderators, and real-world generalizability is needed **before strong conceptual statements and recommendations for application are warranted**." The review's framework (color-in-context theory, per the catalog record and the review's title/abstract surface) holds color effects to be context-dependent — the same hue carrying different meanings in different domains — which is **anti-prescriptive with respect to a context-general hue code**.

**The replication record (mandatory component, recorded here, not in (iv)):** the best-studied single-hue response effect in this base is the red-attraction family. From SRC-0176 (**interior READ this session**, full text): 45 contrasts / 2,961 participants (men rating women) pooled d = 0.26 [0.12, 0.40], falling to d = 0.19 [0.09, 0.29] with a sample-size outlier removed; 36 contrasts / 2,739 participants (women rating men) pooled d = 0.13 [0.01, 0.25]; heterogeneity I² = 89% (men) and 53% (women); a statistically significant relationship between effect size and standard error for women (r = .52, p = .001), read by the authors as indicating upward bias; **a documented decline effect** (more recent studies showing smaller effects, p = .005 for women); and **pre-registered studies for women rating men pooling at d = −0.10 [−0.24, 0.04]** against d = 0.21 [0.08, 0.34] for non-preregistered (p = .03). The two pre-registered replications themselves (SRC-0175, interior unread; results documented both in its abstract and within the read SRC-0176) found little-to-no effect (reported d ≈ 0.09 and ≈ −0.09). The meta was an adversarial collaboration whose authors ended at odds — recorded as a documented disagreement, not adjudicated here.

**Developmental sub-element (age/developmental stage):** SRC-0163 (interior unread; abstract-establishable): in children aged 7 months to 5 years choosing among paired objects, gender-stereotyped pink preference emerges around age 2–2.5 (girls' attraction increasing, boys' avoidance increasing), with no evidence of a special pink preference before ~2 — i.e., the one developmental color-preference result in this base shows preference to be **developmentally emergent, not innate**. Replication status: none recorded in this base. Beyond this single result, **age-stratified evidence on hue-emotion response is ABSENT from this base — stated explicitly.**

**Cultural sub-element:** SRC-0162 documents both a partly universal association core and national/linguistic modulation, **at the association level only**. Cross-cultural evidence on hue **response** (affective, behavioral, physiological) is **ABSENT from this base — stated explicitly.**

**EXPLICIT ABSENCE:** no source in this base measures the prescriptive code's central transfers — that a blue brand asset increases trust responses, that a green asset produces calm, or that a red asset produces urgency perceptions — at any strength, in any population. **The prescriptive code's response evidence in this base is absent; what exists is association evidence plus one small, contested, context-bound response family with an adverse replication record.**

# Element (iv) — Documented Costs and Failure Modes (from the sources; not inferred)

- **Instability of the underlying effects (documented in the read SRC-0176):** the decline effect (recent studies smaller), the preregistration moderator (preregistered women-rating-men studies pooling below zero), indications of upward bias, and stimulus-specificity (the original red shade yielding d = 0.53 vs 0.15 for other reds, outlier excluded) are documented failure modes of the evidence base a hue prescription would rest on.
- **Application prematurity (documented by the field's own review, SRC-0161 abstract):** the review itself states that recommendations for application are not yet warranted — a documented statement that the prescription outruns its literature, made by the literature.
- **Association modulation (documented, SRC-0162 abstract):** color-emotion associations vary by nation and language above the universal core. (The implication of this for any global palette decision is an application question and is **not drawn here**.)
- No further costs of the hue-prescription family are documented in this base. In particular, the business cost of following or unfollowing the code is documented nowhere in the base and is therefore not asserted.

# Source Evaluation
SRC-0178 is a commercial prescription artifact (definitive for what the prescription says; zero authority on whether it works; commercial stake disclosed in its catalog record). SRC-0161 is the field's principal review authored by the red-program's originator (stake disclosed; its "nascent stage" statement runs **against** that stake, which is worth noting descriptively). SRC-0162/0163/0164 are peer-reviewed measurement studies cited at abstract level.

# Assumptions (KOS-0003 §10)
- **Association ≠ response (binding).** Nothing association-level is converted into response evidence anywhere in this claim.
- **The abstract of SRC-0161 is taken as the review's own summary of its field** — the strongest statement assertable without the interior; the interior may contain qualifications in either direction (disclosed).
- **The red-attraction family is taken as informative about the hue code's best case**, because it is the most-studied single-hue response effect in the base — not because it is the code's only content. This is a base-coverage assumption, stated.
- **Terminological bracket (STD-0007):** "association," "preference," and "response" are kept distinct throughout; SRC-0162's "color-emotion associations" are associations with color words, not reactions to color presentations.

# Reasoning (KOS-0003 §7)
**Descriptive reporting with one bounded synthesis step.** Elements (i)/(ii) report a read artifact. Element (iii) reports measured results at the access level actually achieved, with replication status per effect. The synthesis step — "the prescriptive code is not supported at prescriptive strength in this base" — is an absence claim over a fixed base plus the field's own printed self-assessment; it does not claim the code is false, only that its measured support in this base does not exist at the strength prescribed. Reasoning risks checked: (1) **overreading the replication record** — the red-attraction family's troubles do not prove all hue effects fragile; controlled by scoping the inference to "best-studied effect in base"; (2) **motivated debunking** — a dissolution finding is the pre-authorized interesting result, which is itself a convenience prior; checked by recording the universality result (SRC-0162's r = .88 association core) at full strength rather than minimizing it; (3) **association-response conflation** — controlled structurally by the bracket.

# Confidence (KOS-0003 §8)
- **a_prescriptive_approach — Level 3 (Moderate):** the code's content rests on one live-read artifact plus documented wide circulation; the practitioner-manual pole is surface-level only. Not higher: one artifact read.
- **a_stated_rationale — Level 3 (Moderate):** the rationale and its zero-citation trail are directly read; the breadth of that rationale across the practice literature is not directly sampled.
- **a_measured_response_evidence — Level 3 (Moderate):** the load-bearing meta-analysis was read in full and the review's self-assessment is verbatim from its abstract; capped by unread interiors on the association/developmental pillars and by the base's coverage of one response family in depth.
- **a_documented_costs_failure_modes — Level 3 (Moderate):** the principal entries rest on the read meta-analysis; the remainder on abstract-level statements.
- **No Level 4 or 5 anywhere. Everything R0.**

# Limitations
- Asserts nothing about any specific organization, brand, or application; asserts nothing about hue effects outside this base's coverage; does not adjudicate the field's replication dispute; does not claim the hue code is false — only that its measured support in this base is association-level, small, contested, or absent at the strength prescribed.
- **Replication coverage limit:** the adverse replication record documented here binds the red-attraction family specifically; most other cited effects have **no replication information in this base**, which is itself recorded, not treated as reassurance.
- **May not be cited outside Relatio or for any external reliance (R0), regardless of INV-0020's closure state.**

# Alternative Interpretations
1. **"The universal association core (r = .88) vindicates the hue code."** Rejected as stated — the associations are with color terms, are emotion-concept associations rather than induced responses, and include pairings the code does not prescribe; universality of association does not measure any transfer to brand response. Recorded at full strength as association evidence.
2. **"The meta-analysis found significant effects, so red prescriptions are evidence-based."** Rejected as stated — the pooled effects are small, heterogeneous, declining over time, and near zero in the preregistered subset for one direction; the meta's own authors could not agree on interpretation. Recorded as a contested small effect, not a null.
3. **"The hue code fails only because this base is thin."** Partly accepted — the base is eighteen sources and does not represent the literatures; the absence claims are explicitly within-base. What the base does contain, however, includes the field's own review stating application recommendations are unwarranted — which is not a base-thinness artifact.
4. **"Color-in-context theory supports contextual hue prescriptions, so prescription survives in context-specific form."** Not adjudicated — possibly true, but context-specific prescription is a different claim from the context-general code recorded in element (i), and this base does not measure any specific contextual prescription.
