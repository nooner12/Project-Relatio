# Legge & Bigelow (2011) — Does Print Size Matter for Reading? A Review of Findings from Vision Science and Typography

# 1. Identification

Gordon E. Legge and Charles A. Bigelow, "Does print size matter for reading? A review of findings from vision science and typography," *Journal of Vision*, **11(5):8, 1–22 (2011)**, **DOI 10.1167/11.5.8**. A joint review by the principal investigator of the psychophysics-of-reading research program and a practicing type designer, explicitly bridging the measured vision-science literature on print size and the typographic practice tradition. States the **fluent print size** concept — the psychophysically defined range (reported at the surface as roughly 0.2° to 2° of x-height visual angle) over which text can be read at maximum speed — and argues that the distribution of print sizes in historical and contemporary publications falls within it.

Catalogued as a **source base record for the reading-performance investigation prep** under the **ADR-GOV-0003 pattern** (catalog only, grade nothing). **No investigation is opened, no claim recorded, no grade assigned.**
