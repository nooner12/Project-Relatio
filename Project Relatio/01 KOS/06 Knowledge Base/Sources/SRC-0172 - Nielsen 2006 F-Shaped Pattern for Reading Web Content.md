# Nielsen (2006) — F-Shaped Pattern for Reading Web Content

## Draft Source Record

---

# 1. Identification

Jakob Nielsen, "F-Shaped Pattern For Reading Web Content (original eyetracking research)," Nielsen Norman Group article, **published 16 April 2006**, presenting eyetracking visualizations from a study recording how 232 users looked at web pages and describing a dominant F-shaped scan pattern (two horizontal stripes, then a vertical stripe). A 2017 NN/g follow-up article ("F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web: Misunderstood, But Still Relevant") revisits and qualifies the claim; the follow-up is noted here as context, not separately catalogued.

Catalogued as a **source base record for the brand-visual-design response-evidence investigation prep** under the **ADR-GOV-0003 pattern** (catalog only, grade nothing). **No investigation is opened, no claim recorded, no grade assigned.**

# 2. Description (scope note — descriptive only)

A **practitioner-research report**: eye-tracking-derived, but published as consultancy guidance rather than peer-reviewed research. It is the named origin of the "F-pattern" scan-pattern family of layout prescriptions — squarely in the middle stratum the investigation must distinguish (measured attention finding vs. extrapolated rule vs. folklore). (Descriptive characterisation only; no assessment of quality or authority.)

**Stake note (descriptive):** Nielsen Norman Group is a commercial UX consultancy whose business includes selling research-derived guidance; it has a stake in its findings being regarded as prescriptive. Recorded as a fact about provenance, not as an assessment.
