# Elliot & Maier (2014) — Color Psychology: Effects of Perceiving Color on Psychological Functioning in Humans

## Draft Source Record

---

# 1. Identification

Andrew J. Elliot and Markus A. Maier, "Color psychology: Effects of perceiving color on psychological functioning in humans," *Annual Review of Psychology*, **65, 95–120 (2014)**, **DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115035**. The field's principal narrative review of measured color-response research and the statement of **color-in-context theory** — the framework holding that color effects on psychological functioning are context-dependent (the same hue carrying different learned/biological associations in different domains).

Catalogued as a **source base record for the brand-visual-design response-evidence investigation prep** under the **ADR-GOV-0003 pattern** (catalog only, grade nothing). **No investigation is opened, no claim recorded, no grade assigned.**

# 2. Description (scope note — descriptive only)

An **academic narrative review** in a major annual-review venue, co-authored by the leading researcher of the red-effects experimental program (Elliot). It reviews the measured literature on color and psychological functioning and frames it theoretically. (What kind of document it is and what it is about — no assessment of quality, strength, or authority is made or implied.)

**Stake note (descriptive):** the first author is the originator of several of the specific effects the review surveys (the red-attention and red-romance programs); a review of a program by its own originator is not independent of that program. Recorded as a fact about provenance, not as an assessment.
